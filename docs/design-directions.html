The same Monday morning, drawn five ways for the Daylight DC-1 in the kitchen. Every mockup is rendered in grayscale at the screen's 4:3 shape, so what you see is close to what the panel will show. Pick the one that feels like your house, or point at the pieces you want combined.

Type: Old Standard TT for the masthead and headings, Libre Caslon Text for everything else.

What it does well

Looks exactly like what the paper screen wants to be. Calm, dense, and genuinely nice to glance at. A day-specific notice (lunch menu, snow day, birthday) slots in as a boxed "announcement" without changing the layout.

Watch out

It is a reading layout, not a tapping one. The buttons are small and the album picker would have to become its own full page. Kids will read it with help, not drive it alone. The broadsheet look is also a common default right now, so it needs its own details (the ephemeris line, the notice box) to feel like yours.

How a kid uses it

Tap a checkbox to mark a chore. Tap "Pick an album" and a page of six covers appears, laid out like a classified page. The TV list opens the same way.

Type: Fredoka for everything you tap, Caveat for the handwritten notes. The "Draw" button uses the DC-1's stylus.

What it does well

The most child-proof of the five. Four buttons, always in the same place, each with a picture. Cards can be added or taken down (a birthday card, a countdown, a drawing) without the layout caring. It is warm without needing color.

Watch out

This is the direction most likely to get busy. It needs a hard rule: at most five things on the door at once. Rotated cards and hand lettering can tip into kitsch if the execution gets cute; the fix is to keep the lines crisp and the shadows hard.

How a kid uses it

Tap "Watch", get a page of posters grouped by shelf (Shows, Movies, Videos, Podcasts). Tap a poster, a big "Play on the TV" button appears. Done. The "Draw" button opens a blank card that stays on the door until someone clears it.

What it does well

The day-as-a-ruler is the most informative view of time here. A kid can see "school is the long stripe, piano is after". Modules are honest boxes, so adding or removing one is cheap, and it matches Crate's paper theme without any work.

Type: Alfa Slab One for display, Young Serif for text. The illustrations are solid black shapes with white cut-outs, the way a lino print works.

What it does well

Reads from across the room. The black bands give the screen a top and a bottom, so the day-specific slot (lunch menu today, "Happy birthday" another day, "Snow day!" another) has a permanent home in the footer. Big tiles, big buttons, a picture per tile: a child understands it at once.

Watch out

Heavy. The style depends on good illustration, so each new feature needs a drawing, not just a label. Five tiles is the limit; a sixth feature has to replace one or live behind a button. Slab serif at small sizes gets dense, so body text stays in the lighter serif.

How a kid uses it

Tap the tile. "Pick an album" opens a full page of covers, each in a thick frame, four across. Tap one and it starts; the tile updates with the cover. The TV picker is the same page with posters grouped under black shelf labels.

Type: Schibsted Grotesk only, regular and extra-bold. Everything aligns to one grid; nothing is rotated or decorated.

What it does well

Nothing competes. The clock and the timeline do the job a wall clock and a calendar do, and the rest sits quietly below. Lanes per person make "who has what today" obvious. It is the easiest to extend without making it busy, because new things get a column, not a card.

Watch out

Grown-up. A child can use it (round checkboxes, black buttons) but nothing about it invites them. It can read as a corporate dashboard if the type scale is not generous and the grid not loose enough.

How a kid uses it

Same three actions: tap a circle, tap "Pick an album", tap "All shows". Pickers are a grid of covers with a shelf label above each row, in the same single typeface.

My pick

Woodcut for the home screen, Field Guide's timeline inside it. Woodcut is the only direction that gets better on this screen rather than merely surviving it: solid black, hard edges, no gray ramps, pictures instead of labels. It is also the one a child can run alone. Its weakness (five tiles, no more) is a feature for a family dashboard that you are worried about getting busy.

What I would borrow from the others: the Field Guide's hour ruler for the Today tile, because a long stripe for "school" teaches time better than a list; and the Fridge Door's "Draw a note" button, because the stylus is the DC-1's best feature and a kid-drawn note on the kitchen screen is the thing everyone will remember.

If Woodcut feels too loud for the kitchen, Timetable is the safe second choice and the easiest to extend. Fridge Door is the one to pick if the kids' delight matters more than the parents' calm.

Every direction shares one interaction model, so the choice is purely visual: the home screen shows state and has three or four big actions; each action opens one full-screen picker of covers or posters; tapping a cover plays it and returns home. Nothing on the device ever asks for typing. All editing (adding albums, movies, chores, calendar events) happens on your phone.

How it gets built

A native Android app on the DC-1 (it runs Android 13), plus a small backend in the same shape as Crate: Vercel functions and Supabase. The full proposal is in the repo as docs/PROPOSAL.md; this is the short version.

Tablet appKotlin + Jetpack Compose, pinned as a kiosk (lock task mode, screen always on, night clock after bedtime). Talks to the backend over HTTPS and to the TV over the home network directly. One build, five themes are just five Compose theme objects, so switching directions later is cheap.
Backendbrimley-home on Vercel + Supabase, same stack as Crate. Serves one GET /api/today that assembles everything the home screen shows: calendar events, tasks, cards for the day, the TV catalog, and the music state proxied from Crate. Phone-friendly admin pages for editing.
MusicCrate stays the source of truth. The dashboard calls Crate's existing play, state and control routes with a signed-in session for your account. Small Crate change: a "household" token that can only read crates and start playback, so the kitchen never has edit access.
CalendarGoogle Calendar API against the shared family calendar (service account or one OAuth grant). Cached on the backend and refreshed every few minutes.
Tasks and choresNew table. One-off tasks, recurring chores (Mon/Wed/Fri), assignee, done-at. The tablet toggles them; the admin page creates them. Allowance bounties are the same table with an amount.
Day cardsA generic "card" with a show-from and show-until. Your Zo automation posts the lunch menu as a card every Monday morning with a small bearer token (POST /api/cards). Birthdays, snow days and "Grandma arrives today" are cards too, from the admin page.
TVDepends on which Chromecast. A Chromecast with Google TV speaks the Android TV remote protocol on the home network: the app can launch a deep link (a Disney+ title, a Max episode, a YouTube video, a Pocket Casts episode) and send play, pause, volume and back. The catalog is a table of titles with a service and a deep link. An older "dongle" Chromecast can only run Cast receivers, which covers YouTube and podcasts (streamed by RSS) but not Disney+ or Max per title.
Staying currentA subscriptions list (Disney+, Max, Netflix…) you toggle. Titles on a paused service disappear from the picker. A weekly job checks each title's streaming availability through TMDB's watch-provider data and flags anything that moved.
YotoYoto has an official developer API with MQTT control of the player, so "Stories" can start a card on the Yoto itself. If you meant Yoto content on the TV, say so, because that is a different path.

Things I need from you before the build

  • Which Chromecast exactly (the Google TV one with a remote, or the older dongle). It decides the whole TV section.
  • Whether your DC-1 has the Play Store and Google services. I believe it ships with them, which makes Cast and calendar sign-in easier, but confirm in Settings.
  • Whether Zo can call a URL on a schedule (a POST with a token). Its site builder suggests yes.
  • Whether the family calendar is one shared Google calendar or several.

Other ideas

On the two you were unsure about: yes to both, but each gets exactly one slot and stays off the home screen unless it has something to say.

Your idea · recommended, with limits

Bounty board

Not a permanent button. When you post a bounty ("Vacuum the basement, $1"), it appears as a day card. The kid taps "I did it", you approve from your phone, and a small ledger per child keeps the running total. No bounty, no card, nothing on screen.

Your idea · recommended

Our makes

A shelf of family projects behind one button, like a crate for things you built. Sylvie's game can run right on the tablet in a full-screen web view if it works in grayscale, which is a fun constraint to design for. Each project gets a cover, a date, and who made it.

New · the DC-1's best trick

Draw a note

The stylus. A blank card anyone can draw on, which stays on the screen until cleared. "Back at 6", a doodle for Mom, a tally of who won at cards. It is the fridge-magnet drawing, except it can also be sent to your phone.

New

Night clock

After bedtime the screen becomes a large clock and tomorrow's first event, with the amber backlight. In the morning it becomes the dashboard again. The kitchen gets a beautiful clock for free.

New

Big visual timer

A tap on "15 minutes of piano" starts a timer the size of the tile that empties like a sandglass. Kids understand a shrinking shape; they do not understand 14:37.

New

Countdowns

"9 sleeps until Grandma arrives", drawn as a row of little moons. Added from the admin page with a date, shown as a card when within two weeks.

New

Dinner tonight

One line in the header if you keep any kind of meal plan. It answers the question kids ask most, and it can come from a card Zo posts.

New · maybe later

Picture of the day

One family photo a day, dithered to black and white like a newspaper halftone. Surprisingly lovely on this screen, and a reason to walk past it. Needs a photo source you trust (a shared album).

New · maybe later

Screen-time budget

If you want it, the TV picker can show "one show left today" as a row of outlined and filled squares. Same catalog, one extra rule. Skip it if the catalog itself already does the limiting.

Things I would leave out: weather beyond one line, news, email, anything with a feed. The screen should be done changing by the time you finish reading it.
