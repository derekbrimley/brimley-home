The reward, in layers

  • Instant: stamp, burst, sound. Cheap and loud, every day.
  • Weekly: five stars in a week turns the whole band into a "GOLDEN WEEK" banner on Saturday morning, with the cat wearing a crown. Seven stars is a different drawing.
  • Optional, your call: the all-done state could unlock something concrete, like the Watch tile showing "a show is yours tonight". Easy to add later, easy to leave out.

Alternatives if the stamp is not it

  • The cat grows: each completed day the cat drawing gets a little bigger and fatter through the week, resetting Monday.
  • A hidden picture: each completed job reveals one third of a woodcut illustration that changes daily; all done shows the whole thing.
  • A mail slot: all done drops a "letter" into the Note tile that a parent wrote earlier in the admin page ("Ice cream after dinner").

With the dongle Chromecast

This screen works as drawn for Videos and Listen: the tablet casts YouTube and podcast audio directly. For Shows and Movies on Disney+, Max and Netflix, the dongle cannot be told "play this title" by anything except that service's own app. See the plan below for the two ways around that.

Keeping it current

Each movie and show carries a TMDB id. A weekly job checks where it streams, compares against the services you have marked as paid, and marks anything that moved. The admin page shows a short "needs attention" list, and the poster shows a small "moved" corner until you fix or remove it.

Updated plan

Your answers change the TV section and simplify the rest. The repo's docs/PROPOSAL.md is updated to match.

TV, honest versionA dongle Chromecast only runs Cast receiver apps. The tablet can cast YouTube (the YouTube receiver, driven the way the Home Assistant integration does it) and podcasts (the episode's audio URL from the show's RSS feed, into the default media receiver). Disney+, Max and Netflix cannot be started on a given title from anything but their own apps; their Cast receivers only accept playback from an authenticated sender app.
Option A: swap the dongleA Google TV Streamer (or the older Chromecast with Google TV) speaks the Android TV remote protocol on the home network. The tablet can then launch any title on any installed app by deep link, plus play, pause, volume and back. This is the only way "pick Kiki, it plays" works end to end for the paid services. It is the change I would make; it is a one-time cost and the rest of the design stays the same.
Option B: keep the dongleSince the DC-1 has the Play Store, install Disney+, Max and Netflix on the tablet. Tapping a Shows or Movies poster opens that app on the tablet directly at the title (their Android apps accept the same web links), and the kid taps the app's own Cast button once. Two taps instead of one, and the service's app is briefly on screen, so the curation is softer. YouTube and podcasts still go straight to the TV. Workable, not as clean.
Cast on the tabletWith Play services present, the app uses the official Google Cast SDK for Android as a sender, which also gives a proper remote (play, pause, seek, volume, stop) for anything the tablet started.
YotoYoto's developer API exposes your purchased cards with their chapters and audio streams, and MQTT control of the player. So "Pick a story" offers your cards, and a second step asks where: the Yoto player (via its API) or the tablet's own speaker (streams the card). I don't believe the Yoto app can cast to a Chromecast, so the TV is not an option here, and that is fine for stories.
CalendarOne shared Google calendar. A service account is shared on it read-only; no OAuth, no token refresh. "Who" comes from a name prefix in the event title ("Sylvie: piano") or from a per-person keyword list in config.
ZoBackground script, as Zo suggested. Monday 6:00 am, one curl -X POST with a bearer token and the menu as JSON. The backend keeps the last payload so a failed Monday still shows last week's card with a "may be out of date" line. I'll give you the exact script once the endpoint exists.
Google PlayMeans we can ship the app through Play's internal testing track instead of sideloading, and use Play services for Cast and sign-in.

What happens next

If this round is right, the build goes in this order, each step leaving something you can put on the counter:

  1. Shell. The Android app with the Woodcut theme, kiosk mode, night clock, and a stub backend. Real clock, real weather, fake everything else. Installed on the DC-1 by the end.
  2. The day. Calendar and jobs for real, the checkbox, the all-done moment, the admin page for adding jobs.
  3. Music. Crate's household token, the album picker, the now-playing tile and remote.
  4. Cards and the lunch menu. The cards endpoint, the footer slot, the Zo script.
  5. Watch. YouTube and podcasts to the dongle; Shows and Movies via whichever option you pick; the catalog admin and the weekly availability check.
  6. Stories and the note. Yoto, then the stylus note tile.

Two things to tell me: which Chromecast option (swap or keep), and whether the stamp-and-burst is the right "something cool" or you'd rather one of the alternatives.
