The Playing card

One card for music, TV and stories. When nothing is on, it shows "Nothing playing" and the big button. When one thing is on, the cover, where it is playing, and the transport. When two things are on (music in the kitchen and a show in the living room), it splits into two short rows with a small cover each; the button stays. The all-done moment is unchanged: the jobs card inverts, the stamp lands, the stars burst.

Bounties and makes

A bounty shows as the dashed row in the jobs card only while one is posted. "I did it" turns it into "waiting for Dad" until you approve from your phone; then the ledger ticks up. "Our makes" is a button in the Note card, opening a shelf of projects with a cover each; Sylvie's game runs full screen from there.

What I'd still trim

If the screen feels full, the first thing to go is the transport row in Playing (pause lives on the picker instead), and the second is the stars row. The bounty row only appears when there is one.

Where shows come from

You fill the first two columns (Title, Shelf). The app fills "Found on" and a hidden link column every few hours. Blank "Found on" means it stays off the tablet.

How a row becomes a poster on the tablet

  1. You type a title and pick a shelf (Shows, Movies, Videos, Listen). A YouTube link or a podcast name works the same way.
  2. Every few hours the app reads the sheet, looks the title up on TMDB for its poster and facts, and asks where it streams in the US right now.
  3. It keeps only services on your paid list (a short "Services" tab in the same sheet: Disney+, Max, Netflix, Prime). The first match wins and its title link is stored, so the tablet can start it on the TV.
  4. No match: the row stays in the sheet, "Found on" stays blank, and the tablet never shows it. When the title comes back to one of your services, it reappears on its own.

Where the links come from

TMDB (official, free) answers "which services carry this title". JustWatch's data (the same source TMDB uses, through its public but undocumented GraphQL endpoint) also returns the title's page on each service, which is the link the TV needs. The app uses TMDB for availability and JustWatch for the link, and falls back to a plain "open the app" if a link is ever missing. Netflix links are stable ids; Disney+ and Max links are per-title pages that their TV apps open directly.

Same sheet, other tabs

  • Listen: podcast names; the app finds the feed and plays the latest episodes.
  • Jobs: kid, job, days of the week. The tablet keeps the done-state per day.
  • Bounties: job, amount, status. "I did it" on the tablet writes "waiting" into the status cell; you change it to "paid" and the ledger ticks.
  • Makes: title, who, link, picture.
  • Services: the ones you pay for. Delete a row and that service's titles vanish from the tablet on the next sync.

No admin page in the first version. If the sheet ever gets annoying, a table view in the app can read and write the same rows.

The Chromecast

Swap back to the Chromecast with Google TV. The two things you disliked about it are exactly what the dashboard removes:

  • The extra remote goes in a drawer. The tablet sends play, pause, volume, back and "TV off" over the home network, and starts titles directly. The physical remote is only for setup.
  • The home screen never gets shown on purpose. The dashboard deep-links straight into a title, so the kids go from the kitchen screen to Bluey without passing Google's row of recommendations. Two settings help: Google TV's "Apps only mode" hides recommendations if the home screen does appear, and a kids profile restricts what the apps themselves offer.

What it can't fix: when an episode ends, the service's own app is still on the TV with its own "up next" and browse rows. The dashboard can send "TV off" (or back to a blank screen) from the Playing card, and a parent can set a "stop after this" toggle when starting a show, which sends that command when the runtime is up. Netflix and Disney+ autoplay settings in their own apps cover the rest.

With the dongle instead, YouTube and podcasts work from the dashboard and Disney+, Max and Netflix need a second tap on the tablet. The Google TV box makes everything on the Watch tab a one-tap thing, so it is worth the swap.
